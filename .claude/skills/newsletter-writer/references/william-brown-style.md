# William Brown newsletter style — the DNA

This is the structural template the newsletters borrow. It is distilled from
William Brown's (Build, Grow & Exit) email newsletters. We take his **structure,
cadence, and reader-handling** and pour Bjion's voice and opinions into it.

We do NOT copy his persona (the "$40 million / sold to private equity / villa in
Bali" flexing) and we do NOT copy his sales close. See `bjion-voice-and-post-bank.md`
for what replaces them.

## The shape of every William email

1. **Subject line** — curiosity + one concrete, specific stake. Often a number,
   often a confession or a contrarian claim. He is happy to be slightly
   provocative or rough ("The $44,910 mistake your making", "Why I REGRET moving
   to Bali", "The # 1 secret for...").
2. **Preview line** — a tiny parenthetical that deepens the subject's loop:
   "(and how to avoid it)", "(Brutally honest)". Shows in the inbox preview.
3. **Greeting** — first name, casual: "Hey %FIRSTNAME%," or just "%FIRSTNAME%,".
4. **Opening hook (1-2 lines)** — a bold claim, a confession, or a specific
   number that creates an open loop. He commits hard in the first sentence.
   - "Eight months ago, you had the same knowledge that could've made you $44,910."
   - "I thought I'd bought paradise when I dropped a million dollars on my villa... I was dead wrong."
5. **Voice-the-objection** — he says the reader's own excuse back to them, in
   their head-voice, often as short quoted self-talk:
   - "There's that voice talking to you... 'It's not ready yet.' 'Just one more module.'"
6. **The teaching body** — short beats that walk from the problem to the insight.
   This is where a story usually sits. (In Bjion's version a story is replaced by
   an industry observation, a mechanism, or what Navreo sees across clients.)
7. **Proof** — hard, specific numbers and concrete artefacts:
   - "I took a $50 Word document and turned it into $16.4M in sales over 7 years."
8. **The maxim** — ONE plainly-stated, quotable principle, on its own line:
   - "DONE beats perfect every single time."
9. **Sign-off** — "To your success," / then "– Will" / then a standing tagline:
   - "Make Money. Have Fun. Help People."
10. **P.S.** — William uses it for a second pitch. (In Bjion's pure-value version
    the P.S. is optional and must add value or a forward-looking thought, never a pitch.)

## The cadence rules that make it feel like William

- **One to two sentences per paragraph. Almost never three.** Huge amounts of
  white space. The email should read like a staircase, not a wall.
- **Direct second-person address.** He talks TO one reader, and names them
  ("Bjion, here's what's happening..."). Use the merge field `%FIRSTNAME%` once
  or twice in the body, not just the greeting.
- **He voices the reader's internal state** before giving the answer. He earns
  the lesson by first proving he knows where the reader is stuck.
- **Specific numbers everywhere.** Never "a lot of money" — always "$44,910".
  Numbers are the texture of credibility.
- **One idea per email.** Every email lands a single principle. No email tries to
  teach three things.
- **Short, declarative sentences.** Plain words. Conversational, not corporate.

## What we deliberately drop or change for Bjion

| William does | Bjion does instead |
|---|---|
| Personal life stories (Bali villa, ICU, exits) | Industry observation, a mechanism, or what Navreo sees across campaigns. **No personal stories.** |
| Persona flexing ("$40M", "two eight-figure exits") | Practitioner proof: client results, numbers from real campaigns, what actually works |
| Hard sales close ("Book a call, calendar almost full") | **No CTA by default** (see SKILL.md config). Pure value. |
| "To your success, – Will" + "Make Money. Have Fun. Help People." | "Bjion" + the standing sign-off line configured in SKILL.md (none by default) |
| Em-dashes | Never. Commas, colons, full stops, or parentheses. |

## Two annotated skeletons (use as scaffolding, not as copy)

**Skeleton A — the contrarian-claim email** (William's "$44,910 mistake" shape):
```
Subject: [Contrarian claim or specific number that creates a loop]
Preview: ([tiny line that deepens it])

Hey %FIRSTNAME%,

[Bold opening claim, 1 sentence. Commit hard.]

[The cost of the status quo, stated plainly.]

[Voice the reader's objection in their own head-voice.]

[The reframe: here is what is actually happening.]

[Proof: a specific number, result, or example.]

[The maxim — one quotable line on its own.]

[One or two lines bringing it back to the reader.]

Bjion
```

**Skeleton B — the "here is the shift" email** (industry-observation shape):
```
Subject: [Statement that something is changing / dead / about to break]
Preview: ([most do not see it yet])

Hey %FIRSTNAME%,

[The claim: X is changing. State it as fact.]

[Why it used to work / what people still believe.]

[What actually changed, with the mechanism.]

[Proof: numbers, what Navreo sees, a concrete example.]

[What it means for the reader, specifically.]

[The maxim — one quotable line.]

Bjion
```
