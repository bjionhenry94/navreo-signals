## The voice profile (concrete, checkable)

**Greeting** — casual first-name: "Hey [Name]," / "Hi [Name]," / "Hello [Name],".

**Opener / icebreaker — VARIES, never one fixed phrase.** The real emails rotate:
- Self-aware POC apology: "Apologies if this isn't relevant, wasn't sure who at
  [Company] was the best person for this."
- A genuine observation: "Saw you were following [X] and thought you might be doing
  a bit of cold outreach." / "Saw you were working with a number of international
  brands and so wanted to reach out."
- A market-noise line: "We've noticed there's so much noise right now around what
  tools to use for your go-to-market."
The stiff, identical "...so I wanted to reach out." on every email is a robot tell.
It appears sometimes, but it is ONE option among many, never mandatory.

**Middle — there is almost always an elaboration line** the rigid template drops:
"It includes...", "It features...", "We use...". This is where the concrete proof
lives: "$15M+ in pipeline", "30 high-intent leads per month", "over 50 clients",
"scaled to 50 calls a day". Name-drops of well-known brands as social proof.

**The offer / ask — warm and varied, describing the thing is FINE:**
- Lead magnet: "I've put together a brief guide on how wholesalers are using AI to
  find brands looking for new partners... Should I send it over?" — describing what
  the resource covers is normal and good, NOT "second-offer stacking".
- Service pitch: "If we could help your team open conversations with buyers actively
  scoping new suppliers, and you only pay for qualified leads after they show up,
  would you be open to a two-minute video on how we'd do it for [Company]?"
- Pay-after stated plainly: "You only pay after we've built it, so absolutely no
  upfront amount."

**CTA — many forms, all short and warm:** "Should I send it over?" / "Can I send it
over?" / "Can I share it?" / "Could I send a one-pager explaining how it works?" /
"Would you be open to seeing a short video on how we'd do it for [Company]?" — NOT a
single canned "would you be interested in seeing the details?" every time.

**P.S. — common, carries proof or a sweetener:** "P.S. We'll run a pilot for you at
no cost, and if it works we can talk about working together." / "P.S. Last year we
helped over 50 agencies drive $15M+ in pipeline."

**Texture:** contractions everywhere (we've, you're, I've), British spelling,
3-4 short paragraphs, 45-90 words, concrete numbers, a little self-deprecation, warm
not corporate. No em-dashes. Colons appear rarely and are fine.
