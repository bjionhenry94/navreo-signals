## Objection synthesis (what round 2 must fix)

1. **The yes-ladder kills trust.** "If we could... would you be interested?" was named a recognized spam pattern by 4 of 5 panelists. Fix: declarative offers, no hypothetical question.
2. **Hedge words read as escape hatches.** "Typically" flagged twice on A4: "a speed promise with a built-in excuse". Fix: concrete clause or cut the claim.
3. **"Qualified" undefined = hollow guarantee.** All three A2 skeptics: define it in the same breath, pre-agreed list, hard bar ("a soft bar wearing a hard number's clothing").
4. **Anonymous proof is dead weight.** "50+ clients" and the unnamed Europe agency convinced nobody; every panelist asked for one named checkable client. Fix: swap P.S. to the named house claim (calls booked with buyers at Samsung, BCG, HubSpot).
5. **Autonomy scares brand-protective buyers.** "Contacted automatically" read as compliance risk (CRO scored two angles 2/10 on this alone). Fix: an approval clause in every mechanism line ("drafts every email for your sign-off"). True to the product (approve gates exist).
6. **Stack-Native must not assert their stack.** "Your team already uses" was false for 2 of 5 and torched the email. Fix: reframe as no-new-platform ("runs on Claude and ChatGPT"). Packaging note: AIM this angle at AI-tool-adjacent audiences (followers etc.), where the house template already performed 6x.
7. **Mechanism must be named in plain English.** "What's actually doing the work?" from three panelists. Fix: one clause: agents watch hiring/funding/LinkedIn signals daily, build the list, draft the emails.
8. **Build-First's reversal was hollow.** "Paying after it's built means paying for a system that exists, not for a single lead it produces." Fix: tie cost to first qualified replies landing (same DNA as the live June copy "the cost only kicks in when it's actually working").
9. **Free training undercut the DFY pitch** for one panelist. Fix (shared CTA, still identical across arms): frame the training as "the exact method", proof-of-competence, not a DIY alternative.

## Round 2 rewrites (v2)

Shared skeleton v2 (identical across arms). Subject unchanged. New CTA and P.S.:

> Either way, the exact method is in a free training: how to find high-intent leads and contacts from inside Claude or ChatGPT, with a system that runs 24/7 and gets better every week. Can I send it over?
>
> P.S. This same engine has booked our clients calls with buyers at Samsung, BCG and HubSpot.

**A1 Build-First v2:**
> We'd build {{company_name}} a complete outbound engine: AI agents that watch buying signals daily, find the right decision makers, and draft every email for your sign-off. You pay nothing upfront and nothing while we build. The cost only kicks in once it's live and qualified replies are landing.

**A2 30-in-90 Guarantee v2:**
> We'd run {{company_name}}'s outbound with a simple deal: 30 qualified leads in your first 90 days or you don't pay. Qualified means a decision maker from a target list you approve upfront, replying to ask about working together. And you sign off every email before anything sends.

**A3 Stack-Native v2:**
> We'd set up {{company_name}}'s outbound to run on Claude and ChatGPT: AI agents watch hiring, funding and LinkedIn signals daily, build your list, and draft every email for your approval. No new platform to learn, no SDR hire, no agency retainer.

**A4 Live-in-a-Week v2:**
> We'd have {{company_name}}'s outbound live inside a week: by day seven, buying signals are being watched daily and approved emails are reaching real decision makers. If qualified conversations aren't landing by week two, you don't start paying until they are.

Commercial-terms note for Bjion: A4 v2 extends the house "cost kicks in when it's working" mechanic to a timeline backstop. A2 v2 keeps leads (not meetings) as the guaranteed unit, per the brief and honesty rails, but hardens the definition. Both flagged for sign-off at packaging.
