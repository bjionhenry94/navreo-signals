## What round 2 proved

1. **The definition sentence is the strongest line in the whole lab.** "Qualified means a decision maker from a target list you approve upfront, replying to ask about working together" was quoted as the score-driver by multiple judges. It now goes into every payment trigger (A1, A4) in the same grammar.
2. **The magnet pivot is the #1 remaining blocker on A2.** Three separate judges: "pick a lane", "DFY or DIY course?", "a lead magnet wearing a guarantee as a costume". The magnet is fixed by the brief, so v3 welds it: the training is how you SEE THE ENGINE before deciding, not a course beside the offer.
3. **A bare speed claim is radioactive.** Ten of ten verdicts across two rounds scored Speed 6 or below. Experts call 7 days impossible, burned buyers call it tone-deaf, brand-careful buyers call it reckless. v2 never explained WHY a week is credible. v3 leads with the reason (fleet already built, domains already warmed) and adds a consequence floor.
4. **Stack-Native needs "run FOR you" plus a cost anchor.** Non-AI-native buyers hear self-serve babysitting. It also needs the DIY failure modes named (deliverability, list quality, copy), per the judge who burned their own domain trying.
5. **Judges keep asking for price.** No invented pricing allowed; "a fraction of one SDR's cost" is the approved price shape.
