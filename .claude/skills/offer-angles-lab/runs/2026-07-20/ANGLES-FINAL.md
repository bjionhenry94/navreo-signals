# ANGLES-FINAL: four A/B arms for the Claude/ChatGPT outbound offer

Run: 2026-07-20, offer-angles-lab, training mode OFF. Evidence: 4 panel rounds, 20 simulated ICP buyers, 80 verdicts. Zero provider credits spent.

## The honest verdict

**The strict done-rule (all four angles at 8/10+ with 4/5 would-reply) was NOT met.** Peak performance: the 30-in-90 Guarantee hit 7.6 with 4/5 would-reply in round 3, and took 9 of the lab's 12 total yeses. The panel's final gap list is not copy: it is four commercial decisions listed at the bottom. With those supplied, the two lead arms are projected to clear the bar; without them, this file ships the best-achieved versions honestly labeled.

## The law the lab found (worth keeping)

Cold buyers at 5-200 headcount reply to DEFINED-OUTCOME RISK TRANSFER: a number, a deadline, a definition of what counts, and a consequence for missing, written like a contract clause. Every 8+ verdict in 80 landed on an arm carrying those four parts. Positioning stories without them (cost, speed, stack, quality) capped at 4-7 as "interesting, wouldn't reply." The proven old "pay after we've built it" line has the same DNA; the panel simply demanded it be anchored with a count.

The CTA weld ("see exactly how it works before deciding") is the tested fix for the magnet-vs-DFY tension; it stays fixed and identical in every arm.

## Arm 1 (lead): 30-in-90 Guarantee. Best score 7.6, 9 yeses of 20, passed the reply test in round 3

> We'd run {{company_name}}'s outbound on a simple deal: 30 qualified leads in your first 90 days or you don't pay. Qualified means a decision maker from a target list you approved upfront, replying to ask about working together. You sign off every email before it sends, every reply lands in your own inbox, and the monthly price is fixed in writing before anything goes out.

- What converted: the deal sentence itself; the definition sentence; the inbox-counting clause; the price-in-writing clause (quoted as the driver by the industrial CD).
- Open flag (both expert judges, independently): "or you don't pay" plus "monthly price fixed" reads as two different deals. Resolve with ONE line once Bjion decides the mechanics: contingency ("no invoice goes out until the 30th lead lands") OR retainer-with-refund ("billed monthly, refunded in full if we miss 30").
- Deployment rule: right-size the number per niche. 30-in-90 fits volume motions (SaaS, agencies, recruiting). Relationship niches (consulting, industrial, healthcare) read 30 as spam risk or fantasy; quote a smaller fit-vetted number there.

## Arm 2: Build-First risk reversal. Best score 7.0 with a 9 and an 8 from the two most expert buyers

> We'd build {{company_name}} a complete outbound engine and carry the whole build ourselves: buying signals watched daily, decision makers found, every email drafted for your sign-off. You pay nothing upfront and nothing while we build. Billing starts only after your tenth qualified reply, a decision maker from your approved list replying to talk. If they never land, you never pay.

- What converted: the tenth-reply billing clock (quoted as the driver by both yeses; "almost too generous, where's the catch" was the residual objection).
- Approved add-on lines the panel asked for, pending Bjion sign-off (both factually true today): a go-live date ("the engine goes live inside a week") and a brand-safety line ("volumes ramp gradually and nothing sends without your approval"). These answer the zero-stall and domain-safety objections that held three 6s back.
- This arm is the direct descendant of the proven 188k-send template, with the yes-ladder removed and the trigger anchored.

## Arm 3: Cost-Collapse. Best score 5.2. Below bar as a standalone opener; strongest as support

> A loaded SDR runs about $75k a year before they book a single call. We'd run {{company_name}}'s whole outbound for a fraction of that: lists built, every email drafted for your sign-off, sending and follow-ups handled, deliverability protected. No new platform for your team to learn, no hire, no agency retainer, and you only pay while it's delivering.

- Zero would-replies in 20. Experts already know the SDR math; "a fraction" is a number-shaped claim without a number; "while it's delivering" is undefined.
- Where it works: the $75k anchor earned a 7 from the cost-pressured IT MD, and reads well as an EMAIL 2 support paragraph under Arm 1 or 2, or in UK copy quoted in pounds.

## Arm 4: Warm-Replies (signals + compounding). Best score 5.0 (as Speed) / 4.6 (as Quality). Below bar as an opener

> Most cold outbound gets mediocre replies because everyone is blasting the same lists. Ours watches live buying signals (hiring, funding, LinkedIn activity) and only contacts decision makers already in motion, so replies come back warmer. It learns from every reply, so week eight beats week one. You approve every email before it sends, and if replies aren't coming, you're not paying.

- The diagnosis line was praised as the most authentic sentence in the lab, but "it learns" reads as an unfalsifiable slogan and the floor carries no number. Speed as a headline was rejected by 15 straight judges before the pivot.
- Where it works: the signal-watching and learning language is the MECHANISM line inside Arms 1-2 (already partially absorbed). Swap the signal menu per niche: hiring/funding for tech; production lines, facility expansions, tenders for industrial.

## A/B wiring

- Same subject, same opener slot, same welded CTA, same P.S. in every arm; ONLY the middle paragraph swaps. Per-lead {{Icebreaker}} comes from lilly-icebreaker as usual.
- Test Arms 1 and 2 first (the two with yeses); hold 3 and 4 as challenger arms or demote their content to Email 2 support.
- Per-niche deployment: right-size Arm 1's number; swap Arm 4's signal menu; quote currency locally.

## The four decisions that unlock the bar (Bjion only)

1. **The deal mechanics behind the guarantee:** contingency invoice or retainer-with-refund, in one sentence. (Kills the expert-flagged contradiction.)
2. **A price line or price shape** ("from £X/month" or "less than half a loaded SDR"): 4 of 5 round-3 judges said a number gets an instant reply.
3. **The make-good at 22 of 30** (extend free? partial? refund?): one line.
4. **Same-stage proof permission:** one named client at 5-50 headcount with a real number, to use alongside or instead of Samsung/BCG/HubSpot for small-firm niches.
