The slot

Lane 3 is .cards.c23 = 2fr 3fr. The replies card holds the 2fr column; these are built for the empty 3fr one — roughly 700 × 330 at the hub’s 1240px max-width.

Data contract

Nothing new is fetched. The trend is /api/deliverability-trends (series.days / sent / bounce_pct), sliced client-side to 7 / 14 / 30 and sent-weighted. The campaigns are /api/campaign-scorecard (sent, bounced), floored at 500 sends — the floor the hub’s campaign ranking already uses.

What round 1 changed

Five non-technical reviewers scored the first cut; none reached 9/10. What they changed:
  • Today is the hero, not the average. All five said the 30-day average (1.70%, under the limit) buried today’s 2.79%. “I walk away relieved — exactly the wrong outcome.”
  • Ranked by emails that came back, not by rate. Rate order put a 173-bounce campaign above a 742-bounce one.
  • Grouped by client (P2, P3). ValSoft owns 2 of the worst 4 and 55% of all bounces — one phone call, which no prototype said before.
  • Healthy rows cut, jargon cut — “pt”, “sent-weighted”, “re-verify”, “throttled”, “508 / 11,050” all replaced with plain English.
  • One colour, one meaning. Red/amber/green now mean only “where you sit vs 2%”; the trend chip is neutral grey.

Known limit

The scorecard stores lifetime bounced/sent per campaign with no per-day series, so the campaign and client figures do not follow the 7/14/30 toggle — the toggle moves the trend and the average only. Every prototype labels this inline rather than in fine print. Making the list windowed needs a per-campaign daily series that does not exist in the data layer yet.
